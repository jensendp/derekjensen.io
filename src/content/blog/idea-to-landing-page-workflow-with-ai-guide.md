## Step 1: Clarify Your Idea with AI Before You Touch a Builder

Before you open any builder, open a conversation with AI. ChatGPT or Claude both work great here. Think of it like talking through your idea with a smart friend.

Most people skip this step. They jump straight into design and stare at a blank page. That blank page feeling? It comes from not knowing what you're actually offering.

Here's what to do. Paste something like this into your AI chat:

```
I have an idea for [your idea]. Help me answer these three questions:

1) Who is this for? Be specific — describe the person, not just a demographic.
2) What problem does it solve? Focus on the pain they feel right now.
3) What do I want someone to do when they see my page? (e.g., sign up for a waitlist, book a call, buy something)

Ask me follow-up questions if my answers are vague. Push me to be more specific.
```

That's it. Three questions. The AI will ask follow-up questions, and you'll go back and forth for a few minutes. By the end, you'll have a clear picture of your offer.

This is the part of the idea to landing page workflow with AI that saves you the most frustration. When you know who it's for and what it does, the words come easily. The design comes easily. Everything downstream gets simpler. This is the same principle behind [validating ideas without code using AI](https://derekjensen.io/blog/validating-ideas-without-code-using-ai-guide).

Spend 10 to 15 minutes here. It's the highest-value step in the entire process, and it costs you nothing but a short conversation.

## Step 2: Write Your Landing Page Copy with AI (The Right Way)

Here's where most people go wrong. They open ChatGPT or Claude and type something like "Write me a landing page for my business." What comes back is generic, bloated, and doesn't sound like them.

Instead, write your copy section by section. This is the part of the idea to landing page workflow with AI that makes the biggest difference.

> **Warning:** Avoid writing your entire landing page in a single prompt. You'll get generic, bloated copy that doesn't sound like you. Always work section by section so you stay in control of the voice and message.

Start with your headline. Use a prompt like: "Write 5 short headlines for a landing page that helps [your audience] solve [their problem]." Pick the one that feels closest, then ask AI to refine it.

Next, do the same for your subheadline. Then your benefits section. Then your call to action.

Here's a prompt template you can copy and adapt for each section:

```
You are a conversion copywriter. Write the [SECTION] for a landing page.

Context:
- Product/idea: [describe in one sentence]
- Target audience: [who it's for]
- Main problem it solves: [the pain point]
- Desired action: [what you want the visitor to do]

Constraints:
- Keep it under [X] words
- Use plain, conversational language — no jargon
- Sound like a real person, not a marketing brochure

Give me 3 options to choose from.
```

Replace `[SECTION]` with "headline," "subheadline," "3 bullet-point benefits," or "call-to-action button text + supporting line" as you work through each part. For more on writing prompts that actually produce usable results, see the guide on [writing prompts that generate working code](https://derekjensen.io/blog/writing-prompts-that-generate-working-code-guide) — the same principles apply to copy.

This approach works because you stay in control. You're guiding the AI instead of hoping it reads your mind.

Here's a simple prompt structure you can follow for each section:

- **Who** is this for?
- **What** problem does it solve?
- **What** do you want the reader to do next?

Feed those three answers into every prompt and your copy will stay focused.

When you get the output back, read it out loud. If anything sounds stiff or fake, tell the AI: "Make this more casual and direct." You don't need to be a professional writer. You just need to know when something sounds like a real person talking — and when it doesn't.

## Step 4: Assemble and Design Your Page Without Code

Now comes the fun part. You have your copy. You have your builder. It's time to put it all together.

Start by pasting your copy into the builder section by section. Drop your headline in first, then your subhead, then your benefits, then your call to action. Most builders give you a blank template or a starter layout. Either works. Just get the words on the page.

Now make three simple design decisions:

1. **Pick one font.** Stick with whatever the builder defaults to. Seriously. It's fine.
2. **Add breathing room.** If sections feel cramped, increase the spacing between them. White space makes everything look more professional.
3. **Use one image — maybe.** A single relevant photo or illustration near the top can help. But a clean page with no images beats a cluttered page with five stock photos every time.

What about AI-generated images? They work great for abstract concepts or placeholder visuals. Skip them for anything that needs to look like a real person or a real product. People can tell, and it hurts trust.

If you want more guidance on making your page look polished without design skills, the [UI/UX design with AI beginner's guide](https://derekjensen.io/blog/ui-ux-design-with-ai-the-complete-beginners-guide) covers the fundamentals.

This step in your idea to landing page workflow with AI should take 15 to 30 minutes. Don't overthink the design. You can always adjust later. Right now, done beats perfect.

## Step 5: Review, Publish, and Share Your Landing Page

You've done the hard work. Your copy is written. Your page looks good. Now let's get it live.

Before you hit publish, run through this quick checklist:

- **Check mobile view.** Most visitors will see your page on a phone first. Every builder has a mobile preview button — use it. Make sure nothing looks cramped or cut off.
- **Click every link and button.** Does your call-to-action button actually go somewhere? If you're collecting emails, submit a test one yourself.
- **Check load speed.** If your page takes more than a few seconds to load, remove heavy images or swap them for lighter ones.

> **Tip:** Use AI for your final review too. Paste your finished copy back into ChatGPT or Claude and ask: "Read this landing page as if you're a [target audience member]. What's confusing? What would stop you from clicking the button?" You'll catch blind spots you missed.

For hosting, keep it simple and free. Most AI builders like Carrd, Framer, and Replit let you publish on their free tier with a subdomain. That's perfectly fine for validation. You don't need a custom domain on day one.

Now share it. Post it in a group chat, a Slack community, or on social media. Send it to five people who would actually care about your idea. Ask them one question: "Does this make sense?"

This is where your idea to landing page workflow with AI pays off. You built something real in one sitting. Now you get real feedback — the same day.
