Here's what usually happens. You hear about a cool AI tool. You sign up. Then you hear about another one. You sign up for that too. Before long, you're paying for six subscriptions and spending more time managing tools than doing actual work.

This is the tool overload trap. And it's the fastest way to burn out on AI before you even get a real result. If this sounds familiar, you're not alone — there's a whole guide on [how to fight AI tool fatigue and figure out what you actually need](https://derekjensen.io/blog/ai-tool-fatigue-what-you-actually-need-guide).

The truth about figuring out what workflows to automate first with AI? It has almost nothing to do with tools. It starts with picking *one* workflow. Just one.

Not the most impressive one. Not the one that sounds coolest when you tell people about it. The one that's eating your time right now.

When you try to automate everything at once, you end up automating nothing well. Every half-finished setup becomes another thing on your to-do list. Every new tool needs learning. Every integration needs babysitting.

Meanwhile, the real cost adds up quietly. You lose hours to decision paralysis — researching, comparing, second-guessing. You lose money on tools you barely touch. And worst of all, you lose momentum. You start feeling like this whole AI thing isn't for you.

It is for you. You just need a different starting point.

## The 3-Question Filter: How to Choose What Workflows to Automate First with AI

Here's a simple framework you can use right now. When you're trying to figure out what workflows to automate first with AI, ask these three questions about any task:

**1. Is it repetitive?** Do you do this task more than twice a week? Maybe it's copying data between apps, sending the same type of email, or reformatting notes. If it happens on a loop, it's a candidate.

**2. Is it rule-based?** Could you explain the steps to a stranger in under two minutes? "When X happens, do Y." If the task follows a clear pattern, AI can handle it.

**3. Does it drain your energy?** Some tasks aren't hard — they're just annoying. They pull you out of the work that actually matters. Those are gold.

A task that hits all three? That's your starting point.

Here's how to find it. Set a 15-minute timer. Open your calendar and task list from the past week. Write down everything you did more than once. Circle anything that felt tedious or mindless. You'll probably spot your top candidate within five minutes.

> **Tip:** Use this prompt to help you brainstorm candidates. Paste your to-do list or calendar into ChatGPT or Claude and let AI help you spot the patterns:

```
I'm a [your role] and here's a list of tasks I did this week:

[paste your task list or calendar summary]

Based on this list, identify which tasks are:
1. Repetitive (done more than twice this week)
2. Rule-based (follow a clear "when X, do Y" pattern)
3. Energy-draining but not complex

Rank the top 3 candidates for automation, and for each one, explain in plain English what a simple automation could look like.
```

One more thing — the "boring" tasks are almost always the right place to start. Nobody gets excited about automating data entry or follow-up emails. But that's exactly why they work so well. They're predictable, low-risk, and free up your brain for the stuff only you can do.

## The 5 Workflows Most Non-Technical Builders Should Automate First

So what workflows to automate first with AI? Here are the five I recommend to almost everyone.

**1. Content repurposing.** You wrote a blog post. Now you need a LinkedIn post, three tweets, an email newsletter blurb, and a summary for your website. AI can turn one piece of content into five or six formats in minutes. This alone can save you hours every week.

**2. Inbox and lead triage.** Instead of reading every email and sorting it yourself, set up an automation that tags messages, flags hot leads, and even drafts quick replies. You still hit send — but the heavy lifting is done.

**3. Client onboarding.** Welcome emails, intake forms, follow-up reminders — these are the same steps every time. Put them on autopilot so new clients feel taken care of without you scrambling behind the scenes.

**4. Weekly reporting and data summaries.** Pulling numbers from a spreadsheet and turning them into a readable update? That's a perfect job for AI. You get a clean summary dropped into your inbox every Monday morning. If you work with spreadsheets a lot, you might also want to explore [turning a spreadsheet into a web app with AI](https://derekjensen.io/blog/turn-spreadsheet-into-web-app-with-ai-guide).

**5. Scheduling and calendar management.** The back-and-forth of "Does Tuesday work?" is a time killer. Simple automations handle this instantly.

Notice a pattern? None of these are flashy. They're boring, repetitive, and rule-based. That's exactly why they work.

## What Workflows to Automate First with AI — And What to Leave Alone (For Now)

Not everything should be automated. At least not yet.

Some tasks *sound* like perfect candidates, but they actually need your brain — your judgment, your taste, your gut feeling. Knowing what to skip is just as important as knowing what workflows to automate first with AI.

Here's what to leave alone for now:

**Tasks that need your human judgment.** Things like hiring decisions, pricing strategy, or responding to a frustrated client. AI can help you gather info, but the final call should be yours. These moments shape your reputation.

**Your creative process.** If you're a writer, designer, or content creator, don't rush to automate the part where ideas are born. Use AI to help you *distribute* your work, sure. But if you hand off the thinking too early, everything starts sounding the same. Your voice is your edge.

**Anything you don't fully understand yet.** If you can't explain the steps of a workflow clearly, you're not ready to automate it. You'll just automate confusion — and that's worse than doing it by hand. This is a great case for [turning manual workflows into apps with AI](https://derekjensen.io/blog/turning-manual-workflows-into-apps-with-ai-guide) — but only after you've mapped the steps yourself.

Here's what I suggest: make a "not yet" list. Write down the tasks you *want* to automate but aren't ready to. No guilt. No pressure. Just park them.

When the time is right, you'll know exactly where to go next.

## How to Build Your First AI Automation Without Writing Code

Here's the good news: you don't need to write a single line of code to automate a workflow in 2026.

Tools like **Make**, **n8n**, **Zapier**, and **Replit** let you connect apps visually. You drag, drop, and describe what you want in plain English. That's it.

Let's walk through a real example. Say you want to automate content repurposing — one of the easiest wins when figuring out what workflows to automate first with AI.

**Here's how to set it up in Make (step by step):**

1. Create a free Make account.
2. Start a new scenario and pick your trigger. Example: "When a new blog post is published in WordPress."
3. Add a ChatGPT module. Tell it: "Take this blog post and write three short social media posts and one email summary."
4. Add output modules — maybe a Google Doc to collect the drafts, or post directly to Buffer.
5. Hit run. Watch it work.

Total time? About 20 minutes for your first one.

Here's the exact prompt you can paste into the ChatGPT module inside Make:

```
You are a social media content assistant. Given the blog post below, create:

1. Three short social media posts (under 280 characters each). 
   Make each one a different angle — one insight, one question, one bold statement.
2. One email newsletter summary (3–4 sentences, conversational tone).

Blog post:
{{blog_post_content}}

Format the output with clear labels: "Social Post 1:", "Social Post 2:", "Social Post 3:", "Email Summary:"
```

You'll make mistakes. That's normal. The interface is forgiving, and you can test each step before anything goes live. ## FAQ

The most common ones are surprisingly simple. Content repurposing is huge — people take one blog post and let AI turn it into social posts, email newsletters, and short summaries. Email triage is another big one. AI sorts your inbox, tags messages by priority, and even drafts replies. Beyond that, client onboarding, weekly data summaries, and scheduling are all popular. The pattern? Most successful automations aren't flashy. They're boring, repetitive tasks that used to eat hours every week.

### How do you choose which tasks to automate with AI first?

Run every task through three questions. Is it repetitive? Is it rule-based? Does it drain your energy? If you answer yes to all three, that's your starting point. When figuring out what workflows to automate first with AI, focus on low-risk, high-frequency tasks. Something you do every day or every week — like sorting leads or formatting reports. Save the complex, high-stakes stuff for later, once you've built some confidence.
